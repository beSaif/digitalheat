1. The problem
In DigitalHeat's words: "Servers turn almost all the electricity they use into heat, and that heat is usually cooled away with even more energy. At the same time, homes spend money and energy to produce heat." Which jobs fit, how to schedule them against a building's heat demand over a year, and when it pays off is still open.

2. Our solution
A scheduler that runs computing jobs only where a building needs heat right now, and a console for DigitalHeat to run the fleet. Customers order finished results (embeddings, search reranking, 3D rendering) and never see a server. The heat goes into the host's tank.

Reference house (Basel, 2025 weather, 11 servers: the fewest that cover all of its heat with its 35 kWh tank): 99.97% of the 31,231 kWh heat demand covered (the model starts 1 January with an empty tank), no heat dumped, 40.5% utilisation (13.8% from April to September), 0 missed deadlines out of 47,799 jobs. Electricity costs CHF 12,982 a year and the heat is worth CHF 4,153. Break-even is CHF 0.23 per server-hour, or CHF 1.64 with hardware. Sold at AWS spot price (CHF 2.74 per server-hour) the house makes a profit of CHF 43,073 a year after electricity and hardware. It stops paying off below CHF 1.64 per server-hour or below 23% utilisation.

3. How it works, and what is real
Every hour, for every building: free room in the tank plus heat demand decides how many servers may run (0.8 kW of heat each). Rerank jobs (due in 4 h) go first, to the building with the most free heat. Embed jobs (due in 48 h) fill the rest. When the heat stops, a job pauses if heat comes back within 2 hours. Otherwise it saves a checkpoint and goes back in the queue, possibly to another building.

Real:
- Embeddings (all-MiniLM-L6-v2) and reranking (ms-marco-MiniLM-L6-v2) on BEIR SciFact run on our laptops. We measured runtime and energy on three Macs (M1, M3, M4) and scaled them to the 1 kW reference server.
- The same scheduler code that makes the yearly balance pauses, resumes and checkpoints a real running job (python3 -m scheduler.live).
- In the live demo, people scan a QR code and their phone or laptop becomes the server rack of a building. The jury orders a job, it is split into parts, and only racks whose building needs heat at that moment get parts. The 3D rendering and both AI jobs really run on those devices.

Mocked or assumed:
- No real servers or tanks. In the live demo the tanks are simulated and run 60 times faster than real time.
- The 24-house Swiss fleet in the console is a scenario replayed on 2025 weather, not telemetry.
- Job arrivals (4 rerank jobs per house per day, unlimited embedding backlog), deadlines and the prices of other services are our assumptions. All of them are listed in docs/assumptions.md.
- Heat demand uses the case's formula and one weather year.
