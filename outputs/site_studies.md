# Site and scheduler studies

Generated by `python3 scripts/site_studies.py` (Basel weather 2025, same scheduler as the yearly balance). Scenario numbers, not measurements. The reference-house key figures are unchanged by everything here.

## 1. Daily curves: homes vs businesses

Mean weekday profile ÷ the site's own daily mean (1.00 = flat). Plot: `plots/daily_profiles.png`.

| Site | Jan peak/mean | Jul peak/mean | Peak hours (Jan) |
|---|---|---|---|
| family | 1.66 | 2.05 | 06h, 07h, 08h |
| office | 1.61 | 2.18 | 06h, 07h, 08h |
| pharma | 1.17 | 1.23 | 06h, 07h, 08h |
| hospital | 1.40 | 1.70 | 06h, 07h, 08h |
| spa | 1.22 | 1.30 | 09h, 19h, 20h |

Equal-energy mix in January: family + office peak/mean **1.59**, family + office + pharma **1.45**; July: 1.98 and 1.73. Measured profiles (BDEW) show homes and offices both boost at 06-08. They complement each other afterwards: homes have a second peak 17-21 h when offices are in setback, offices hold the daytime when homes dip, and offices drop at weekends while homes do not. Mixing just the two flattens the peak only a little; adding a 24/7 site (pharma reheat, hospital, spa) flattens it more. The larger complement is seasonal: sites with summer heat (pharma, spa, hospital) keep servers busy when homes and offices do not (section 3).
